About Me

From Bijni to the Big Bang

A small-town boy from Assam chasing the universe's biggest secrets — one collision at a time.

The Origin Story

Bijni, Assam

I am Rohit Agarwala — a PhD Scholar in High-Energy Nuclear Physics at the Department of Physics, Bodoland University, Kokrajhar, Assam. I hail from Bijni — a beautiful, warm little town in Chirang district, Assam, nestled amidst the verdant landscapes of the Bodo heartland. It is a place I carry in my heart wherever I go.

Growing up, I was far from a prodigy. Mathematics — the very language of the universe — was my Achilles heel up until Class 7. I was an average student who struggled with numbers, and the thought of becoming a scientist seemed impossibly distant. But then, life sent me exactly the teacher I needed.

The Man Who Changed Everything

Mr. Manoj Dutta

"

In 2013, when I was in 8th standard, I began taking private mathematics tuitions under Mr. Manoj Dutta — and that decision changed the entire trajectory of my life.

Before him, mathematics was a source of fear and frustration. He didn't just teach me formulas and theorems — he rewired the way I thought. He showed me the beauty hidden beneath the numbers, the logic threading through every equation, the sheer elegance of mathematical reasoning. Under his patient guidance, I went from struggling with basic arithmetic to genuinely loving mathematics.

By the time I reached 11th standard (2016), that love for mathematics naturally evolved into a deep fascination with Physics — because physics, at its core, is mathematics made physical. The equations of motion, the wave functions, the field theories — they all sang to me in a language Mr. Manoj Dutta had taught me to understand.

I scored well in Physics in my 12th boards, went on to pursue a B.Sc. in Physics, then an M.Sc., and now a PhD. Every paper I write, every conference I attend, every equation I solve — it traces back to that one teacher from Bijni who believed in a below-average student and gave him the greatest gift: the love of learning.

"The best teachers don't give you answers — they give you the courage to find them yourself."

— A lesson I carry from Mr. Manoj Dutta's classroom to every physics lab in the world
My PhD Supervisor

Dr. Kalyan Dey

If Mr. Manoj Dutta gave me mathematics, then Dr. Kalyan Dey gave me my research identity. I first encountered him during my M.Sc. at Bodoland University, where his courses on Nuclear Physics and Computational Physics were nothing short of revelatory. His pedagogical depth and genuine enthusiasm for high-energy physics made those classrooms feel like windows into the very fabric of the universe.

Dr. Dey supervised my M.Sc. dissertation on forward-backward multiplicity correlations in pp collisions using PYTHIA8 at LHC energies — my first real taste of research. By 2024, the decision was natural: I enrolled in the PhD program under his supervision on 15th February, 2024.

Now in the third year of my PhD, with many ups and downs behind me, I can say with conviction — I am exactly where I am supposed to be, doing exactly what I love.

The Research Journey

Where I Am Now

My research revolves around the most extreme conditions in the universe — recreating the state of matter that existed mere microseconds after the Big Bang. Using Monte Carlo simulations (PYTHIA8, HYDJET++, UrQMD, AMPT, EPOS4), I probe correlations, fluctuations, and collective phenomena in proton-proton and heavy-ion collisions at RHIC and LHC energies.

The tagline "A Researcher Who Goes With The Flow" is a physics pun I wear proudly — because my work literally revolves around radial and anisotropic flow. But it also reflects my philosophy: to stay curious, stay adaptable, and let the science take you wherever it needs to.

Beyond Research

Teaching & Mentoring

I am passionate about education and mentoring the next generation of physicists. I have taught at Kendriya Vidyalaya Kokrajhar, Bijni College, and currently serve as an Assistant Faculty at Bodoland University — teaching Digital Electronics, Nuclear & Particle Physics, and Computational Physics. I believe that great teachers shape great scientists, and I aspire to be for my students what Mr. Manoj Dutta was for me.
